## THE PROBLEM

### Headline

**The most expensive word in a service business is "fine, just give them a discount."**

### Body

It happens at the front desk, during checkout, in the middle of a complaint. A customer is unhappy. The appointment ran long. The staff member wants to smooth things over. So they offer 20% off.

And they do it again tomorrow. And the day after.

No one tracks it. No manager approves it. No report flags it. By the time you notice, you've given away thousands of rupees in margin that you'll never recover – to customers who may not even have deserved it.

Discounting isn't the problem. Uncontrolled discounting is.

---

*Three-panel problem block:*

**🎁 Discounts as a habit, not a tool**
When staff can apply any discount at any time, it stops being a customer retention tool and starts being a reflex. "10% off" becomes the default response to every complaint, every delay, every awkward checkout moment.

**📉 Margin erosion you can't see**
You won't find a line item called "discounts given without approval" in your weekly numbers. It hides inside revenue. You see lower totals. You assume it's a slow week.

**🚫 No accountability, no pattern**
Without a request record, you can't see which staff members discount most, which services get discounted most, or which branches are most exposed. You're managing blind.

---

## THE SOLUTION

### Headline

**Every discount starts with a request. Every request gets a decision.**

### Body

When a staff member wants to apply a discount, they submit a request – the percentage, the booking, the context. A manager reviews it. If it's approved, it goes through. If not, it doesn't.

Simple. Structured. Logged.

And because every applied discount is tied to a booking, a staff member, a service, and a value in NPR – you have a full audit trail. Not just for compliance. For understanding where your margins actually go.

---

## THE WORKFLOW

### Headline

**What discount control looks like in a real service business.**

---

**The customer asks for a discount**
A client mentions the wait was long. The staff member wants to make it right. Instead of applying 20% and hoping no one notices, they submit a request – service, booking, reason, and the percentage they think is fair.

**The manager reviews in real time**
The branch manager sees the request. They decide: is this justified? Is this within policy? They approve or decline. The staff member gets the answer. The customer gets a clear outcome.

**The discount is applied – with a record**
If approved, the discount is applied to the booking. The NPR amount is calculated and logged. The booking's final amount reflects it. The payment records it. The audit trail is complete.

**At the end of the month**
You can see every discount given across every branch. Who requested. Who approved. What percentage. What value. Which services. Which customers. Pattern recognition, not guesswork.
